# Blanc fmint deviations from OpenZeppelin `ERC20FlashMint`

Blanc's `fmint` is a proof-oriented ERC-20 token with the
[ERC-3156](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-3156) flash-mint extension. It is
**not** a source, storage, or bytecode model of any deployed contract. This
registry catalogues observable semantic differences between `Blanc/Fmint.lean`
and the OpenZeppelin reference below. As with
[`WETH_DEVIATIONS.md`](WETH_DEVIATIONS.md), *the comparison is behavior* — it is
not a claim that the two implementations share storage layout, source, or
bytecode.

**Claim hygiene.** Nothing in this file is a proof. The conservation invariant
(`totalSupply = Σ balances`) is **unproven pending Arc B** of
`~/plans/flashmint-proposal.md`; the `flashLoan` success specification is Arc C.
No solvency claim is made or implied — during a flash loan the minted supply is
unbacked by construction, which is the point of the mechanism. No liveness claim
is made: fixtures are specification-checked differential testing on chosen
inputs, per [the fixture suite's README](scripts/fixtures/fmint/README.md#what-the-suite-establishes).

**Events are adjudicated by this suite, with one stated limit.** Jaune's
runner recomputes the receipts root and the logs bloom from its own execution
and fails the block on either mismatch, so each fixture's committed
`receiptTrie`/`bloom` pins fmint's exact emitted logs — address, topics, data,
order, per transaction. The limit is provenance: those golden values are
`run_t8n` output, the frozen EELS oracle executing our own bytecode, so the
check is differential plus a golden regression lock rather than independent
evidence that the D6 event set is the *right* one. Mechanism, the tampering
experiment that establishes it, and the retraction of Step 2's contrary "rig
finding" are in
[`scripts/fixtures/fmint/README.md`](scripts/fixtures/fmint/README.md#how-this-suite-commits-to-events).
This governs every `Fixture evidence` cell that mentions an event (rows 12,
13, 14).

## Checked similarities

Recorded here as the OpenZeppelin source walk (Step 3) confirms them — items
compared directly against `ERC20FlashMint.sol`/`ERC20.sol` at the pinned tag
where no observable divergence was found, in the style of
[`WETH_DEVIATIONS.md`'s own such section](WETH_DEVIATIONS.md#checked-similarities).

- **Boolean return values.** Every state-changing entry point that OpenZeppelin
  declares `returns (bool)` — `approve`, `transfer`, `transferFrom`,
  `flashLoan` — returns exactly `true` on success in both implementations;
  neither ever returns `false` (OpenZeppelin's `ERC20` guidelines note this
  explicitly: "functions revert instead [of] returning `false` on failure").
- **The ERC-3156 magic constant and every cross-contract selector.**
  `RETURN_VALUE = keccak256("ERC3156FlashBorrower.onFlashLoan")` and all four
  ERC-3156 selectors (`flashLoan`, `onFlashLoan`, `maxFlashLoan`, `flashFee`)
  were independently computed through Blanc's `dynBytes`-extended selector
  machinery and checked against the published EIP-3156 values at Steps 0–1;
  they agree bit-for-bit, and three of the four exercise `dynBytes`'s
  rendering of `bytes` in a real signature, not merely a synthetic one.
- **No fallback or `receive` function.** `ERC20FlashMint`/`ERC20` declare
  neither; Solidity's default for a contract with no fallback and no `receive`
  is that a call to an unrecognized selector, or a bare Ether transfer, reverts
  outright. fmint's dispatcher miss routes to `Func.rev` — an explicit bare
  revert (`Blanc/Fmint.lean:199-203`) — reaching the same observable outcome
  (revert) by an explicit branch rather than the compiler's implicit one.
- **The mint bound arithmetic.** OpenZeppelin's `maxFlashLoan` and fmint's
  agree not just on output (row 2) but on the reason it prevents overflow:
  both frame the bound as "the largest amount that cannot push `totalSupply`
  past `2^256 − 1`," and neither needs a separate overflow check at the mint
  site because the bound check already establishes it.
- **`totalSupply`'s accounting semantics.** Storage *location* differs (row
  17), but the *value* both report tracks net mint-minus-burn identically:
  OpenZeppelin's `_update` increments `_totalSupply` on mint and decrements it
  on burn; fmint's mint/burn pairs do the same to the supply slot. No case in
  either implementation's design produces a supply value the other's semantics
  would disagree with.
